Dead claim: c6 Amortized over one season, the robot's cost per bed planted is lower than the hired-labor cost per bed
Cause: ungroundable
declared hole: No source supports the comparison, and the grounded numbers imply its opposite by roughly two orders of magnitude. The only garden-bed-scale robot on the market costs $7,995 (s1, pricing section) and covers 18 square meters, about 4 standard 30-inch beds (s1, dimensions section), so one-season amortization is roughly $2,000 per bed before installation and upkeep. Hired labor at the $17.15/hr US median (s3) costs under $20 per bed even at a deliberately generous full hour per bed, and a push seeder finishes a bed in one or two walking passes (s4), so the real figure is lower. Field-scale robots amortize worse here: $60,000 (s6) against a 20-to-30-hectare design target (s5) is 80 to 120 times the plot in question.
